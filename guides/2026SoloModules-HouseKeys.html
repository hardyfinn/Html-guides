House Keys

Derrick May, Kevin Saunderson and Mr. Fingers. It does not use samples of those records. It models

the five machines the sounds came from, and plays real factory voice data where that exists.

One engine plays at a time. Pick a sound and play it from V/OCT and GATE, up to 16 voices.

Quick start

  1. Patch a polyphonic V/OCT and GATE (from MIDI-CV, or a sequencer) into V/OCT and GATE.
  2. Patch L and R to your mixer. If you only patch L, it carries the mono sum.
  3. Turn SOUND, or press an engine button to jump to its first sound.
  4. Leave GRIT at the middle: that is the real machine.

Right-click menu

  • Polyphony limit: 4, 6, 8 (default) or 16. Most of these machines had 6 or 8 voices, and the limit

is part of how they sound when chords overlap.

  • Fixed velocity when VEL is unpatched: default is 100, or 64 for FM sounds because the

DX100's keys had no velocity.

  • Load 4-operator voice bank (.syx): any 32-voice bank for the DX100, DX21, DX27, DX11 or

TX81Z. Then Play a voice from the bank. The display shows USR and the voice name. It stays

until you turn SOUND, and it is saved with the patch.

The five engines

FM (sounds 1 to 30). A model of the Yamaha DX100: four operators on its sound chip, with that chip's

floating-point converter. The 30 sounds are real factory voices, read from the original voice banks, so

Solid Bass is the Solid Bass. LatelyBass is the TX81Z's, on that synth's chip. GRIT goes from a clean

converter (0) to the DX100's (0.5) to a rougher one (1).

POLY (31 to 54). Roland Juno-60, Juno-106, Jupiter-6 and JX-8P, plus the Korg Poly-800. Oscillators,

sub oscillator, the resonant 4-pole filter and envelopes. The Juno and JX oscillators are digitally

controlled and hold pitch; the Jupiter's drift slowly. The Poly-800 has one filter shared by all its

voices, so a new note retriggers the filter for everything that is sounding, and that is modelled.

PD (55 to 66). Casio CZ phase distortion: a cosine bent into saw, square, pulse and resonant shapes,

two lines that can be detuned against each other (the Reese bass is two detuned saw lines), and the

CZ's stepped amplifier and 8-bit output.

WAVE (67 to 88). Ensoniq SQ-80 and ESQ-1: three oscillators reading 8-bit waves, sync and ring

modulation, an analog 4-pole filter, four envelopes and three LFOs. The waves are rebuilt by

synthesis; Ensoniq's own wave data is not in the module.

SAMPLER (89 to 100). The Ensoniq Mirage: 8-bit memory, a low sample rate per sound, no

smoothing between samples, and an analog 4-pole filter. The sources (strings, piano, choir, brass)

are physical and spectral models "recorded" into that memory when you choose the sound. The

memory and playback do most of the work of the sound.

Patch recipes

  1. Solid Bass riff with TWIN. Sound 1, OCTAVE 0, TWIN about 0.3, CHORUS OFF. A 16th-note
  2. sequence around A1. TWIN gives the two-synths-layered width of many 1988 records without
  3. smearing the attack.
  4. Jazz Organ stabs. Sound 10, STAB on MIN7, VELOCITY 100%. Play single notes on the off-

beats; the chord follows in parallel, the way a sampled stab did. Try TAPE at 0.2.

  1. Reese bass. Sound 55, OCTAVE −1 if your sequence sits at C2. Long notes, DECAY to the right.

Raise TONE slowly for the movement. Sound 56 next to it shows the bass with no beating.

  1. Mr. Fingers bass and chords. Two House Keys: one on 31 Feel Bass, one on 32 Feel Chords.

Feed both into a mixer and put TAPE around 0.35 on both. Chorus on the chords only. This is

the "Can You Feel It" pairing: a cheap mixdown to cassette is part of the sound.

  1. Life Strings and Life Piano. Sound 89 on long held minor chords, sound 93 on a second

module playing the stabs. GRIT 0.5. For the darker, grainier take use 91 Low-Rate Strings

instead of 89.

  1. SQ-style pad. Sound 75 Glint Pad or 76 Breath Pad, ATTACK to the right, MOD at 0.2 for a little

vibrato, CHORUS I. Slow chords, polyphony 8.

  1. Cassette-era setup. Any sound, GRIT at 0.75, TAPE at 0.6. The tape's saturation takes the top

off loud notes first, which makes stabs sit back in a mix.

  1. Loading a 4-operator voice. Right-click, Load 4-operator voice bank (.syx), choose a DX100,

DX21, DX27, DX11 or TX81Z bank, then Play a voice from the bank. The display shows USR and

the name. Turn SOUND to leave it.

  1. Poly-800 shared filter. Sound 52 800 Strings. Play a held chord, then add a note: the filter

envelope restarts for every sounding note, as on the real Poly-800.

  1. Sound per step. Sequence the SOUND CV (0 to 10 V spans the 100 sounds). Sounds change

after a 20 ms fade, and held notes restart on the new sound.

What is modelled and what is estimated

This page is honest about where the module is exact and where it is a best estimate.

Exact, or very close - The 30 FM voices are the original factory voice data, decoded and written to a

model of the chip's registers. The chip core is ymfm (Aaron Giles, BSD licence), an accurate

emulation of the Yamaha OPM-family chips. - Note pitch on FM is within 2 cents of the real machine

(tested). The operator frequency-ratio table and voice decoding are tested against the documented

values. - STAB chord intervals, polyphony limits and voice stealing behave like the hardware.

Modelled from published circuits and measurements, with estimated constants - The DX100

converter's exact noise and stepping (GRIT 0.5), and the TX81Z's. - The Roland filter, oscillators and

chorus rates and delays. Chorus rates are from published Juno-60 measurements (tested within 2%).

  • The Jupiter-6's oscillator drift; the Poly-800's shared filter. - The CZ's stepped amplifier and its

converter. - The SQ-80 envelope time and LFO speed curves. One known doubt: with the current

LFO speed curve, the delayed vibrato in Analog Strings, Formant Voices, Reed and Wave Sync Lead

runs at about 0.6 Hz. That is a slow wobble, more than a vibrato. It is one constant and may change

after review. - The tape stage (wow, flutter, saturation, head bump, hiss) is a general cassette model,

not a particular deck.

Rebuilt, not copied - The SQ-80's waves and the Mirage's samples are Ensoniq's and are not in the

module. The waves are rebuilt by synthesis; simple shapes are very close, sampled attacks (pick,

slap, chiff) less so. - The Mirage sources (strings, piano, choir, brass, orchestra hit, upright bass,

vibes) are models of the instruments, recorded into a model of the Mirage's 8-bit memory. They are

not the Mirage library disks. - The POLY, PD, WAVE and SAMPLER sounds named after records are

built from the documented machine and how it was used. They were not fitted to the records by

measurement. Clips of the sounds that matter most would let them be fitted.

Loudness: every sound is trimmed so one note at velocity 100 peaks at the same level, so you can

flick through sounds without riding the fader.
