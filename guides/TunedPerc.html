The idea

Tuned Perc plays one percussion sample in key. When you load a sample, it listens to it, works out its pitch, and shifts it to the nearest C. V/OCT then plays it like an oscillator: 0 V is the drum's own C, +1 V is an octave up, and +7/12 V is a fifth up. A tom, 808, rim or bell can follow the same sequence as your bass or chords.

Getting started

  1. Right-click and choose Load sample..., or drag a WAV or AIFF from Finder onto the module.
  2. The screen shows the detected note (for example E2+12c), how sure it is, and the C it has been moved to (C2, -4.12 st).
  3. Patch a trigger into TRIG, a sequencer's pitch into V/OCT, and OUT to your mixer.

Controls

ControlWhat it does
OCTAVE-3 to +3 octaves.
FINE-50 to +50 cents.
DECAY10 ms to 4 s. Fully CW plays the whole sample.
LEVELSilent to +6 dB. The default (about 2 o'clock) is 0 dB.
TUNEto C: use the detected pitch. as is: play the sample as recorded, so you can compare. V/OCT works either way.
PITCHhold: V/OCT is read when each hit starts, like a drum machine. track: the hit follows V/OCT while it plays, for glides and bends.
DETECTListen to the sample again (lit while working). Use it after changing Listen to in the menu.

Jacks

JackWhat it does
TRIGEach rising edge plays the sample from the start. A new hit fades the last one out in 3 ms.
V/OCT1 V per octave. 0 V = the drum's own C.
VEL0-10 V scales the level. Unpatched = full.
OUTAudio, 5 V peak. Stereo files are mixed to mono.
ENVThe hit's envelope, 0-10 V, for sidechain ducking or a filter.
NOTEThe note actually playing, as standard V/OCT (0 V = C4). Patch it into an oscillator to tune a synth to the drum.

The screen

  • Top line: the file name and how sure the detector is. Below 40% it is red, and the sample plays as recorded ("no clear pitch").
  • Big line: the detected note and cents, then the C it is moved to and by how many semitones.
  • Waveform: the teal band is the part the detector listened to (the click at the very start of the attack is skipped). The yellow line is the pitch over time (+-1 octave fills the height), so an 808 glide shows as a falling line.
  • Bottom line: the note playing now, and the OCTAVE and FINE settings.

How the pitch is found

Most drums don't repeat their waveform exactly: a tom, bottle, can or tabla rings at a few resonances that aren't whole-number multiples of each other, and the sound bends and fades while it rings. Since version 2.2 the detector listens to the spectrum instead:

  1. It skips the click of the attack and cuts the rest into overlapping frames (up to 190 ms long, so even a 30 Hz kick is placed to a few cents; shorter for sounds that only ring briefly).
  2. In each frame it finds the resonances: sharp peaks that stand well above the noise around them. A frame counts as pitched when most of its energy sits in resonances.
  3. The frame's note is the strongest resonance, or the fundamental of a harmonic series when the peaks form one: a bright saw lands on its root, and a bass hit whose fundamental is weak or missing still gets the right note.
  4. The answer is a loudness-weighted median of the pitched frames. How sure it is depends on how much of the sound is pitched and how well the frames agree.
  5. If that finds nothing, the older waveform-repeat method (YIN) gets a second try, which suits very short synth blips.

Noisy sounds (hats, snares, claps) don't have one clear pitch, so they are left alone. The shift is never more than 6 semitones, so the character of the drum changes as little as possible.

Right-click menu

  • Load sample... and drag and drop: WAV or AIFF, 8/16/24/32-bit or float, any sample rate, up to 20 seconds.
  • Next / Previous sample in folder to audition a folder quickly.
  • Listen to: which part of the hit counts.
    • Settled pitch (default): ignores the first quarter of the pitched part, where an 808 or a tom is still gliding down. Usually what you hear as the drum's note.
    • The whole hit: everything with a clear pitch.
    • The attack: the start of the pitched part, for drums whose note you hear mostly at the top of the glide.

The sample's path is saved with the patch, and the pitch is detected again when the patch opens. After changing Listen to, press DETECT.

Patch ideas

These follow from the controls above.

  • Drums that follow the bassline: send the same sequencer pitch to a bass oscillator and to Tuned Perc's V/OCT, with a tom or 808 loaded. Trigger from the sequencer's gate.
  • Tune a synth to a drum: patch NOTE into an oscillator's V/OCT so it plays the note the drum is sounding.
  • Ducking: use ENV as a sidechain or filter modulation source.
  • Bends: set PITCH to track and patch a glide or LFO into V/OCT for dives and bends during the hit.
  • Accents: patch an accent or random CV into VEL.
