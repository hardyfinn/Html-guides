Buchla 259t for VCV Rack

A west-coast complex oscillator after the Buchla / Tiptop 259t, with a modulation oscillator, principal oscillator and wavefolding timbre section. By Haer.

What it does

A west-coast complex oscillator laid out after the Buchla / Tiptop Audio 259t: one oscillator that plays another. Original DSP and panel by Haer; no Buchla or Tiptop artwork is used. Both oscillators are polyphonic (up to 16 channels) and 4x oversampled, so heavy folding and deep FM stay clean.

MODULATION OSC → (AMPL / PITCH / TIMBRE × MOD INDEX) → PRINCIPAL OSC → TIMBRE · SYMMETRY · ORDER → FINAL

Independent implementation. Buchla and Tiptop Audio are not involved; the names say only what it is modelled after. The 259t's ART tuning system is not modelled.

Quick start

Patch FINAL into a VCA or low-pass gate (there is no envelope on board) and a sequencer's pitch into the principal's V/OCT. With TIMBRE at zero you hear a plain sine. Raise TIMBRE and the sine folds into something brighter and more vocal. Then switch PITCH on in the modulation section and raise MOD INDEX: the modulation oscillator now FMs the principal.

Controls

Modulation oscillator

ControlWhat it does
FREQ / FINEA0 to A8; FINE is ± a fifth.
RANGEHI is audio rate; LO is an LFO, 0.1 to 27 Hz.
WAVESaw, square or triangle. The shape that modulates the principal and comes out of WAVE and CV.
CV AMT / FM AMTAttenuverter for the CV input (1 V/oct at full) and depth of the audio FM input (linear, through zero).
InputsV/OCT, CV, FM.
TRI, WAVE, CV, GATETriangle; the WAVE-switch shape (±5 V); the same shape unipolar 0–10 V; a gate high for the first half of each cycle.

Modulation routes

ControlWhat it does
MOD INDEX (+ CV)How hard the mod osc works on the principal, for all three routes. Bipolar: left of centre flips the modulation. CV: 5 V = full.
AMPLAmplitude modulation of the principal.
PITCHFrequency modulation, linear through zero by default (right-click for exponential).
TIMBREThe mod osc moves the TIMBRE amount, so the folding opens and closes at the mod osc's rate.
PHASE LOCK + STRENGTHPulls the principal onto a harmonic of the mod osc, or of whatever is patched to LOCK IN (which replaces the mod osc as the source). STRENGTH sets how firmly.
GATE (principal)The principal as a gate, high for the first half of each cycle.

Principal oscillator

ControlWhat it does
FREQ / FINESame ranges as the mod osc.
InputsV/OCT, CV (with amount), audio FM (with amount).
SINE, SQUAREThe principal's sine and square, after FM and lock.
FINALThe shaped, modulated output.

Timbre section

ControlWhat it does
TIMBRE (+ CV amount)How much shaping is applied. TIMBRE CV: 10 V = full sweep.
SYMMETRY (+ CV)With ORDER left: offsets the wave before folding, bringing in even harmonics. With ORDER right: sweeps the shape from sawtooth to an "M" pseudo-square. CV: 5 V = full sweep.
ORDER (+ CV)Crossfades the two shapers: left is a wavefolder, right is the sine-to-saw/M shaper; halfway mixes both. CV: 10 V = full sweep.

Right-click menu: exponential FM on the PITCH route (default is linear, through zero).

Patch ideas

Folded bass

Principal around A1, sequencer into V/OCT, FINAL into a VCA with a short envelope. TIMBRE about 30%, and the same envelope into the TIMBRE jack so each note opens bright and closes to a sine.

Harmonic FM bells

Same V/OCT into both oscillators, mod osc tuned to a fixed interval above. Whole-number ratios give organ and brass tones; non-integer ratios give bells. PITCH on, envelope into MOD INDEX.

Slow self-moving texture

Mod osc in LO range at a few Hz, TIMBRE and AMPL routes on. Send a slow random voltage into ORDER and SYMMETRY.

Phase-locked drone

Detune the principal near a harmonic of the mod osc, LOCK on, STRENGTH around 60%. Sweeping the principal FREQ steps between harmonics instead of beating.

Polyphonic chords

Send poly V/OCT into the principal and a poly envelope into TIMBRE for plucked, glassy chords; keep TIMBRE low.

Percussion

Principal low, PITCH on, mod osc 1.5–3 octaves up with a high index and a short envelope into MOD INDEX and a VCA.
