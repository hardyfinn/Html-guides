Joanna (rhyming slang: "the old joanna" in the front room or the pub) is a physical model of one particular upright piano: its strings, felt hammers, dampers, pedals, soundboard and cabinet, how long it is since the tuner came, how much it has been played, how old it is, and the room it stands in. It aims at the sound of a home piano for jazz and gospel, not a concert grand.

Every piano has a serial number (the brass plate). It fixes every quirk of every key: each key's tuning error, the spread of its strings, hammer hardness, key weight, damper quality and noise. The same number is always the same instrument. ANOTHER builds a new one.

Quick start. MIDI-CV (polyphonic, 16 channels) → V/OCT, GATE and VEL. MIDI-CC with CC 64 → SUS for your sustain pedal. L and R to your mixer. Then try the presets (right-click). Middle C is 0 V. Unpatched VEL plays at 6 V. VEL 0 V presses a key silently: the damper lifts and the string can ring in sympathy.

Controls

ControlWhat it does
SIZESpinet to tall old upright: string lengths (short bass strings are stiffer and sound "smaller"), the break between wound and plain strings, the lowest soundboard mode.
HAMMERHammer felt from soft and voiced to hard; past 85 % it becomes a tack piano (drawing pins in the felt). Loud notes get brighter on their own, because felt stiffens as it is squashed.
TOUCHHeavy action (you have to dig in) to light (everything comes out loud).
SUSTAINHow long the strings ring. The prompt sound and the long quiet aftersound both scale.
SYMPStrings answering each other through the bridge: held keys, the pedal-down halo, EXT. 50 % is the physical amount.
NOISEKey thump on the keybed, keys and hammers coming back, dampers landing, pedal knocks and swish, a squeaky pedal and a rattle in the room when WEAR is high.
TUNINGTime since the tuner came, from today to 20 years. Each key drifts on its own, the middle of the keyboard drifts away from the ends as the soundboard moves, the strings within a note drift apart and beat, the whole piano sags flat (about 30 cents at 20 years), and after about 8 years a few keys have one wild string.
UNISONExtra spread between the 2 or 3 strings of each note, up to 25 cents: honky-tonk without waiting 20 years.
WEARHow much it has been played: hammers hard and flat where people play most (the middle), uneven voicing and key weight, loose action noise and slight timing slop, rattles.
AGEHow old it is: dead, tubby bass strings, duller treble, a tired soundboard with a weak "killer octave" around C5, dampers that leak.
FINE±50 cents. Right-click for concert pitch A = 440, 435 (old pitch) or 432.
LIDShut (boxy, dark), top open, or front panel off (bright, close, more action noise).
WALLThe back of the piano from 3 cm to 60 cm from the wall: close gives a fat, slightly hollow bass.

ROOM / FURNISH Box room, front room, up to a school or church hall; carpet, curtains and a sofa up to bare boards.

DISTANCEInside the piano (mics over the hammers, bass left and treble right), the player's seat, across the room, then through the door into the next room.
PEDALSSUSTAIN lifts all dampers (SUS CV 0-10 V adds to it, so half pedalling works). SOFT moves the hammers closer (upright half-blow: quieter, softer). FELT drops the practice felt between hammers and strings.
CV ±5 VHAMMER (read at each key press), TUNING, DIST. EXT feeds outside sound into the bridge: sing or play a drum into it with the pedal down.
OutputsL and R through the room. DRY is the soundboard and cabinet only, mono, for your own reverb.

Jazz and gospel starting points

Sunday morning. Preset 4 (Sunday service). Play wide voicings with the bass an octave and a fifth below; pedal on each chord change (lift just before, press just after the chord). Raise SYMP to 65 % for more halo between changes.

Late set. Preset 5 (Late set). Shell voicings (3rd and 7th) in the left hand stay clean with the pedal up. Velocity from 3 to 7 V; HAMMER at 50 % keeps the top round.

Old upright on a record. Preset 3 (Gran's piano) with DISTANCE at 0.25: close enough to hear the action, far enough for the room. Turn TUNING up slowly while a loop plays and hear the piano go out of tune under your fingers.

From your rig. Send Hapax chords over MIDI into VCV's MIDI-CV (poly). For movement that never repeats, put a slow random voltage (Marbles style) into TUNING CV at a small amount, or a sample-and- hold into HAMMER CV per chord. For arrangement contrast, automate DISTANCE: verse from the next room, chorus at the seat.

Silent keys. Hold a chord with VEL at 0 V (no hammers, dampers up), then hit staccato notes elsewhere: the held chord rings out of nowhere. Great with a gate sequencer on the staccato notes.

How it works

Strings are modal: every partial of every string is its own resonator, at the exact frequency of a stiff string (fk = k f0 √(1+Bk²)), so the partials stretch upwards as on a real piano. The tuning is stretched the way a

tuner does it, by matching octaves by ear on this piano's own strings. The 2 or 3 strings of a note are coupled through the bridge, which produces the beating of slightly mistuned unisons and the two-stage decay (loud prompt sound, then a long quiet aftersound) without being programmed in. The hammer is a mass on a nonlinear felt spring, solved sample by sample, so it can hit a string that is still ringing. Hard blows on the bass make "phantom" partials and the metallic longitudinal clang through tension modulation. Every string listens to the bridge, which gives the sympathetic resonance. A 48-mode soundboard, the cabinet and lid, the wall behind and a shoebox room (12 reflections per ear plus an 8-line reverb tail) finish the sound.
