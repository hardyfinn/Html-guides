Quick start. Patch a clock or sequencer gate into HIT and MIX to your mixer. Press the red HIT button to audition. Patch a velocity CV into VEL (Marbles X, a sequencer's accent lane, Random8). Add RIM and XSTICK triggers for the other two strokes. Right-click the module for 20 presets (listed below). Loading one leaves SYMP and LEVEL as they are.

What is inside

Nothing here is a sample or a noise burst shaped to sound like a snare. It is a small physical drum, in real units (metres, kilograms, newtons), and the sound comes out of the parts pushing on each other:

  • Two heads. The top head rings at about 180 frequencies at once and the thin bottom head at about 50. They sit at the spacings a real round drum head has (Bessel zeros), not a harmonic series. That is why a snare sounds like a drum rather than a note.
  • The air inside joins the two heads. Hit the top and the trapped air pushes the bottom head out. This is the only way the bottom head, and so the snares, get moving, exactly as on a real drum. DEPTH changes how much air there is.
  • Pitch drop. A hard hit stretches the head, so for a moment it is tighter and higher, then it falls back as it dies away. Soft hits barely bend.
  • The stick is a small mass that flies into the head and bounces off. Hard hits bounce off sooner (about 2 ms instead of 5), which is what makes them brighter. With BUZZ the fingers keep pressing and the stick bounces many times.
  • Snare wires are 8 strips of coiled wire pressed against the bottom head by the strainer. When the head moves away faster than the wires can follow, they lift off and slap back. Those collisions are the buzz, and each slap also rings the wire coils (the bright fizz). They also deaden the bottom head, just like real snares.
  • The rim is a metal hoop with its own ring. A rimshot hits head and hoop together. In a cross-stick, the stick itself rings like a wooden bar.
  • Shell and mics. The shell rings a little (more when metal). The TOP and BTM outputs are close mics, and MIX adds the drum heard from a little further away.

Controls

SIZEHead diameter, 10 to 15 inches. Bigger drums have more air and a deeper body.
DEPTHShell depth, 3 to 8 inches. Shallow = tight and papery (piccolo), deep = fat, more air between the heads.
TUNETension of the batter (top) head, shown as the pitch it would have on its own. V/OCT adds to it.
RESOPitch of the thin snare-side head relative to the top one. Higher is crisper and more sensitive.
DAMPA gel pad near the edge. Kills the ring (overtones) first, then the body.
SHELLWood (short, warm) to metal (longer, brighter ring).

POSITION Where the stick lands: centre is fat and dry, the edge rings with overtones.

STICKHard nylon/wood tip to a soft felt mallet.
BUZZAfter the hit, the fingers keep pressing the stick into the head, so it bounces several times: a buzz / press stroke. 0 = an open stroke.
RIMFor rimshots: how much of the stick lands on the hoop versus the head.
HOOPTriple-flanged steel hoop (ringy) to die-cast (heavier, drier crack).
XSTICKCross-stick: how far the stick sticks out past the rim. Short = high click, long = low wooden knock.
SNARESThrow-off: snares on or off (the SNARES gate input overrides it when patched).

TENSION Snare strainer. Loose = long, sloppy, sensitive buzz. Tight = short and crisp.

WIRESNumber of strands, 12 to 42. More strands = denser, heavier buzz.
SYMPHow hard the EXT input pushes on the heads (sympathetic buzz).
MICWhat the MIX output listens to: top mic to bottom mic (the room is always in it).
LEVELOutput level.

Jacks

HITTrigger: stick on the head.
RIMTrigger: rimshot (tip on the head and shaft on the hoop at the same moment).
XSTICKTrigger: cross-stick (stick lying on the head, the butt end knocks the hoop).
VEL0 to 10 V, read at each trigger (7.5 V if unpatched). Sets the stick's speed, 0.3 to 7.5 m/s.
SNARESGate: when patched, high = snares on, low = off.
EXTAudio from outside (a kick, a bassline) pushes on the heads, so the snares buzz in sympathy, like a real snare next to a bass amp.

V/OCT, POS, STICK, BUZZ, TENSION, DAMP CV. ±5 V covers the whole knob range. POS, STICK and BUZZ are read at each hit.

TOP, BTM, MIXTop mic over the batter head, bottom mic under the snares, and a mix that adds the whole drum in the room.

Patch ideas

  1. A drummer, not a drum machine. Clock into Marbles. Use t1 for HIT, t3 (sparser) for RIM, and X1 into VEL with SPREAD around 11 o'clock. Ghost notes happen by themselves when the velocity dips. Send X2 into POS for a hand that never lands twice in the same place.
  2. Press rolls on demand. Fast ratcheted gates (Pam's at ×4 or ×8) into HIT, with a slow ramp into BUZZ CV. The roll goes from separate strokes to a closed buzz. Try TENSION low for a loose, sloppy roll.
  3. Sympathetic buzz (Autechre territory). Send your kick or bassline into EXT, with SYMP at noon and no triggers at all. The snares buzz only when the bass hits the drum's own resonances, so it follows your bassline's notes. Then add sparse HITs on top.
  4. Tuned snare. Sequence V/OCT from Metropolix and set DAMP high and SNARES off for a tuned tom / timbale. Flip the SNARES gate from a Random8 output to throw the snares on and off per bar.
  5. Mixing. Use TOP and BTM like a real engineer does: TOP for body and crack, BTM (quieter, high- passed around 300 Hz) for the wire fizz. Engineers often flip the bottom mic's polarity: try it (an inverting VCA or mixer) if the blend sounds thin.

Presets (right-click)

  1. Maple 14×6.5, all-round The default. A good medium snare for anything.
  2. Brass 14×5, bright and crisp Metal shell, higher tension, tighter snares: classic bright backbeat.
  3. Piccolo 13×3.5, high and tight Small and shallow. Cracks, sits on top of busy patterns.
  4. Fat 14×8, low and loose Low tuning, deep shell, loose wires. Big and wide.
  5. Dry and tight (funk) Heavily muffled and tight. Short, punchy ghost notes.
  6. Open and ringy, no muffling No gel at all: long ring and overtones, sloppy snares.
  7. Detroit backbeat (tight brass) Tight brass with a firm hand near the centre, for straight 4/4 techno and house.
  8. 80s big snare (ringy, roomy) Low and ringy with lots of room in MIX. Try a gate after it.
  9. Steel 14×6.5, cutting rimshots All-metal and ringy hoop. Use the RIM input.
  10. Marching (very tight, deep) Very high tension, many tight wires, hard tip.
  11. Jazz, soft and sensitive Soft stick and loose snares. Ghost notes sizzle.
  12. Press roll (buzz strokes) BUZZ is up: every hit is a press stroke. Fast gates give a roll.
  13. Tom-tom (snares off, low) Snares off, low and deep: a floor tom.
  14. Timbale (snares off, metal, high) Snares off, high metal shell, ringy steel hoop.
  15. Field drum (deep, felt mallet) Big and deep, played with a felt mallet. Military or orchestral.
  16. Cross-stick ballad (woody knock) Long stick overhang for a low woody knock. Use the XSTICK input.
  17. Lo-fi trashy (loose, slack wires) Small, damped, slack wires everywhere and a bottom-heavy mic.
  18. Sizzle (bottom mic, all the wires) All the wires, bottom mic: nearly all fizz.
  19. Popcorn 10\" (tiny and very high) A tiny 10-inch drum tuned very high.
  20. Dusty breakbeat (dead, mid) Very dead and mid-tuned, like an old sampled break.

Starting values

Detroit-style backbeat: preset 7, or Brass with TENSION 0.7, POSITION 0.25, VEL 6 to 8 V on 2 and 4 and 2.5 to 3.5 V for ghosts. Big and roomy: Fat preset, MIC at 0.6. Rimshot crack: RIM 0.6, HOOP towards CAST.
