DistantPlanet

VCV Rack 2 multimode filter by Haer: three audio inputs, one 12 dB filter, one output (8 HP)

A Steiner-Parker style filter in the spirit of the Tiptop Audio Forbidden Planet. Most multimode filters have one input and several outputs. This one is the other way round: LP, BP and HP are three ways into the same 2-pole, 12 dB filter, and the input you patch sets the mode.

How it works

LP IN + BP IN + HP IN  →  one 12 dB filter  →  VOLUME  →  OUT
  • A sound into LP is low-passed, into BP band-passed, into HP high-passed.
  • Patch two or three inputs at once and they are filtered together. The same sound into LP and HP gives a notch. All three at low RESO gives a flat response, and the filter only shows once you raise RESO.
  • Different sounds in different inputs (say bass into LP, hats into HP) share one resonance and one sweep, so they move as one voice.
  • Raising FREQUENCY removes more of the HP input and lets more of the LP input through, so an HP sweep feels backwards. That is normal.
  • RESO self-oscillates as a sine from about 80 %. Near 100 % it gets wild and unstable. Loud inputs tame the resonance, as on the hardware, so turn the source down for more ring.

Panel

ControlWhat it does
FREQUENCYCutoff, 20 Hz to 20 kHz (large knob, top).
RESOResonance (default 40 %). Self-oscillation from about 80 %, wild near 100 %.
VOLUMEOutput level. Noon = unity.
1V/OCT (in)Frequency CV at full strength, calibrated so the self-oscillation plays in tune.
CV (in) + AMTA second frequency CV through the AMT attenuator (trimpot). At full AMT it is 1 V/oct.
LP / BP / HP (in)Audio inputs into the low-pass, band-pass and high-pass parts of the filter. Use any combination.
OUTThe single output.

Layout, top to bottom: FREQUENCY; RESO and VOLUME; 1V/OCT, CV and AMT; LP, BP and HP inputs; OUT.

Polyphony: everything is polyphonic, and OUT has as many channels as your busiest input. Stereo: merge L and R into a 2-channel cable, patch it in, and split OUT.

Patch ideas

  • Moving notch: the same chord into LP and HP (use a Mult), RESO about 50 %, a slow LFO into CV with AMT raised. The notch sweeps through the sound.
  • One filter, two parts: bass into LP, hats or noise into HP, an envelope from the bass into CV. Both parts share the resonance.
  • Sine voice: nothing patched in, RESO around 82 to 85 %, pitch from a sequencer into 1V/OCT, then a VCA with an envelope after OUT.
  • Unstable drone: RESO 95 to 100 %, a very slow random CV into CV with a small AMT, a quiet drum loop into HP to push the oscillation around.
  • Thin acid line: a saw bass into BP with RESO around 70 % and an accent envelope on CV.
