SQ10izer for VCV Rack

SQ10izer is a 20 HP sequencing utility by Haer. It takes a clock and a V/OCT pitch, and on every clock adds a small, unquantised offset (up to ±40 cents) from one of 16 steps. A perfectly quantised sequencer then plays slightly out of tune in a repeating way, like an early analogue step sequencer where every step's pitch was dialled in by hand and no two steps were ever quite in tune.

Unlike an LFO, which drifts smoothly and never repeats, each step has its own fixed tuning that comes back every loop.

The idea

Your sequencer sends notes that sit exactly on the grid. SQ10izer keeps its own row of 16 pitch offsets and moves one step on every clock. On each step it adds that step's offset to the incoming pitch:

OUT = V/OCT IN + (step knob × STRENGTH)

A cent is 1/100 of a semitone. At around 5 cents you get a faint shimmer; at 40 you get a properly drunk sequencer.

The panel

ControlWhat it does
Steps 1–16One knob per step, from −100% (flat) through in tune (noon) to +100% (sharp). How many cents 100% means is set by STRENGTH. Hover a knob to read its offset in % and cents; double-click to put it back in tune. The light under each knob shows the playing step; steps inside the loop glow faintly.
LENGTH + CVWhere the pattern loops, 1 to 16 steps (default 16). The CV adds 1.5 steps per volt.
STRENGTH + CVHow far the pattern pulls away from in tune: 0 to 40 cents, starts at 20. It acts live on all 16 steps at once. The CV adds 4 cents per volt.
RANDOMISE + TRIGButton and trigger input. Sets all 16 step knobs to new random positions. The knobs really move, so you can fine-tune by hand afterwards. The button lights briefly.
CLOCKEach trigger moves on one step. Use the same clock as the sequencer feeding V/OCT IN.
RESETBack to the start: the next clock plays step 1. A clock arriving within 1 ms of a reset is ignored.
V/OCT INThe pitch to wobble. Polyphonic is fine: every voice gets the same offset. With nothing plugged in, OUT carries the offsets on their own.
OFFSETJust the step offset as a voltage, with no input pitch added.
OUTV/OCT IN plus the step offset, with the same number of channels as the input.

Rack menu commands

  • Randomize (Ctrl/Cmd-R) randomises the steps but leaves STRENGTH and LENGTH alone.
  • Initialize puts every step back in tune.
  • Bypass passes V/OCT IN straight to OUT.

The playing step is saved with the patch.

First patch

  1. Send your clock to your sequencer and to SQ10izer's CLOCK.
  2. Sequencer pitch into V/OCT IN, and OUT into the oscillator's V/OCT. Gate and envelope as usual.
  3. If the sequencer has a reset out, send it to RESET so step 1 lands on the first note.
  4. Set STRENGTH to about 10 to 20 cents, press RANDOMISE until it sounds right, then nudge single steps.

LENGTH against your sequence

If LENGTH matches your sequence length, each note always gets the same tuning. If it does not match (for example a 16-step sequence with LENGTH 7 or 5), the same notes come round with a different tuning each pass, which slowly shifts the feel without changing the melody.

Patch ideas

  1. Basic SQ wobble. As in the first patch.
  2. Out-of-phase loops. LENGTH that doesn't match the sequence (sequence 16, SQ10izer 7 or 5).
  3. Drift over a track. A slow LFO or envelope into STRENGTH CV: start in tune, let the wobble creep in during a breakdown, take it out again for the drop. The pattern stays the same, only its depth changes.
  4. Mutating pattern. A slow clock divider (every 8 or 16 bars) into RANDOMISE TRIG: the tuning pattern re-rolls now and then while the notes stay put.
  5. Chords that beat. A poly chord into V/OCT IN moves the whole chord together. For a looser chord, use OFFSET into a second oscillator's fine-tune or FM input instead.
