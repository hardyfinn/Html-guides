New in 2.4.0: Cymbolism Lite, the same cymbal with size, weight, rivets and beaters held inside a range that stays light however it is set or randomized. It has its own manual.

What it is

Cymbolism computes a cymbal from its shape. You set the type, size, thickness, taper, bow profile, bell, flange and alloy; it works out the cymbal's real vibration modes (often more than two thousand of them up to 20 kHz) and then plays them with a beater that physically touches the metal. Nothing is sampled.

How it works, in plain words:

  • The modes. The cymbal is treated as a thin shell of revolution: a curved bronze disc whose thickness and curve change from the bell to the edge. A small finite-element solver finds every mode up to the top of hearing, with its shape and frequency. This happens on a background thread whenever you change the geometry; the old cymbal keeps ringing until your next strike, and then the new one takes over.
  • The bloom. Hit a cymbal hard and its big bending motion stretches the metal, which couples every mode to every other. Energy pours upwards over tens to hundreds of milliseconds: the swelling wash of a crash, and the small pitch glide at the start. The lower modes (up to roughly 800 Hz at HIGH) run as a fully coupled nonlinear shell. Above them, the flow of energy up through the spectrum is modelled statistically, calibrated against the full model, because coupling every high mode costs about ten times real time.
  • The beaters. Stick tip, shoulder, mallet and rods are masses with a nonlinear contact spring that land on the moving surface. The brush is a spray of tiny wire impacts. The bow is a friction law at

the edge that locks onto one or two modes and sometimes jumps between them, as a real bowed cymbal does.

  • The rest. The rivets are loose masses that bounce on the surface and buzz. CHOKE is a hand grabbing the edge. DAMP is tape on the bow. MOUNT is the felt and wing nut, including how far the cymbal swings on the stand. The mic is a model of how sound radiates from the shell to a mic position, so moving it changes the tone the way moving a real mic does.

Strikes arrive about 8 to 15 ms late when the engine runs on its own thread, the default; it adapts to Rack's audio buffer. Turn "Run on its own thread" off in the context menu for about 1.5 ms of latency, at the cost of putting the whole load on Rack's audio thread.

Turning TYPE loads that type's shape (weight, taper, profile, bell, flange) into the knobs, unless "Keep geometry when TYPE changes" is ticked in the menu. "Load the type's playing defaults" sets PLAY, POSITION and SIZZLE to suit it.

Controls

Cymbal (changing these rebuilds the modes, about a second)

SIZEDiameter, shown in inches. Size is an exact scale of the whole cymbal, so it needs no rebuild and can be modulated freely. Its CV is 1 V/oct: +1 V halves the size and raises every pitch an octave.
WEIGHTThickness. Thin is low, washy, quick to bloom and very nonlinear; heavy is higher, pingy and glassy.
TAPERCentre thickness over edge thickness. More taper gives a crashy edge around a solid, rideable centre.
PROFILEHow high the bow rises. Flat is dry and controlled; high is brighter, with more pitch glide and a stiffer low end.
BELLBell size and height: from no bell to a big, loud, pitched bell.
FLANGEEdge turned down (negative) or up (positive, china). Large effect on trashiness.
ALLOYB20 is dark and long; B12 and B8 are brighter and shorter; brass and nickel silver sound cheaper; steel is harsh and long.
HAMMERFrom a perfect machine-spun disc to a hand-hammered one: splits mode pairs (slow beating), detunes modes and twists their orientation. No rebuild. NEW CYMBAL re-rolls the hammering

pattern: a different cymbal of the same model. It is saved with the patch.

TENSIONResidual stress from hammering and lathing. Positive stiffens and raises the low modes; negative loosens them. Too much negative would buckle the cymbal, so it is limited automatically.

Sound (no rebuild)

DECAY0.25x to 4x the natural ring time.
BLOOMThe nonlinearity: 0 is a linear, glassy, bell-like cymbal; 1 is physical; 2 is an exaggerated, roaring wash.
DAMPTape or a gel pad on the bow.
MOUNTTight felt (shorter, drier, damped lows) to loose (longer low ring, and the cymbal swings on its stand after a hit, which slowly shifts the tone at the mic).
SIZZLE0 to 12 rivets.
MICA continuous morph: BELL (close over the bell), BOW (close over mid-bow, default), EDGE (bright and trashy), OVERHEAD (about 1 m up), ROOM (3 m away, with air absorption). Each position is matched in loudness.

Playing

PLAYTIP, SHOULDER, MALLET, RODS, BRUSH, BOW. The PLAY CV (0 to 10 V across the six) is added and read at each strike.
POSITIONWhere the beater lands: bell (0) to edge (1). The bell gives a clear bell tone; the edge drives the full crash.
FORCEStrike speed, 0.2 to 12 m/s, when VEL is unpatched; with VEL patched, the speed at 10 V. It is also the bow pressure and the EXCITE gain.
HARDNESSBeater stiffness within its type: soft wood to nylon, soft yarn to hard felt, soft to stiff brush wires.
RATE2 to 30 Hz: roll speed for the struck beaters, sweep speed for BRUSH, bow speed for BOW.
EXCHow much of the EXCITE input is pushed into the cymbal at the strike point.
STRIKE / CHOKEHit now; grab the cymbal while held.

Jacks

STRIKE (trigger), VEL (0 to 10 V, read at the trigger), CHOKE (gate), ROLL (gate: rolls with the struck beaters, a sweep with BRUSH, bowing with BOW), EXCITE (audio), PLAY, and CV for every knob that has a jack (CV adds to the knob; 10 V covers the full range). OUT is mono, about 2 to 3 V peak for a medium crash hit; very hard hits are softly limited above 8 V.

Context menu

Quality:

  • ECO: 47 nonlinear core modes, 16 kHz band, about 19% of a core.
  • MID: 56 core modes with coupled upper pairs and the full band, about 27%.
  • HIGH (the default): 64 core modes and coupled upper pairs, about 37%.
  • ULTRA: 96 core modes and more pairs, about 85%.

These figures are for a loud, ringing crash, measured on a cloud machine, so expect less on an Apple silicon Mac. A big CHINA costs about a quarter more, and SIZZLE at 12 adds about 15 points. The engine rests when it can: once a ring has faded far enough that the bloom no longer matters, the cost falls to under 10%, and a silent cymbal costs nothing until the next strike.

The other menu items:

  • Run on its own thread.
  • Keep geometry when TYPE changes.
  • Rebuild on geometry CV changes. When this is off, geometry CV is read only at strikes.
  • Load the type's playing defaults.
  • OUT trim.

Patch recipes

1. Jazz ride

  • Setup: RIDE, TIP, POSITION about 55%.
  • Pattern: a swing pattern from any sequencer into STRIKE and VEL, with accents on the downbeats.
  • Bell hits: send a gate-shaped CV into POSITION, so that chosen hits land on the bell (−5 V moves a hit from mid-bow to the bell).
  • Tone: MIC between BOW and OVERHEAD.

2. Big crash and choke

  • Setup: CRASH, SHOULDER, POSITION 95%, FORCE high, MOUNT loose so it swings.
  • Choke: patch a second trigger, a beat later, through a short envelope into CHOKE.

3. Orchestral swell

  • Setup: HEAVY RIDE or CRASH, MALLET, HARDNESS about 35%, RATE about 15 Hz, MIC OVERHEAD.
  • The swell: hold ROLL high and ramp VEL from 0 to 10 V over a few bars, then release ROLL and let it ring.

4. Brushes

  • Setup: CRASH RIDE, BRUSH, POSITION about 70%, RATE 5 to 8 Hz.
  • Playing: gate ROLL for sweeps and send triggers to STRIKE for taps.

5. Bowed metal

  • Setup: CHINA or BELL, BOW, ROLL held high.
  • Movement: a slow LFO into RATE and FORCE makes the bow jump between modes.
  • Pressure: high pressure with a slow bow chokes the tone, as a real bow does.

6. One cymbal, many cymbals

  • Setup: sequence SIZE and WEIGHT CV per hit, with a few distinct settings.
  • Why it works: the last 8 shapes stay cached, so returning to one swaps instantly. SIZE alone never needs a rebuild.

7. Drum loop bloom

  • Setup: a drum loop into EXCITE, EXC about 70%, CHINA or CRASH, BLOOM 1 to 2.
  • Result: the kicks and snares bloom into metal.

8. Sizzle and tape

  • Setup: SWISH or RIDE with SIZZLE 4 to 8 and DAMP to taste.
  • Variation: try MOUNT loose for a lazy shimmer.

9. Impossible cymbals

  • BLOOM 2, STEEL and the thinnest WEIGHT on a big size.
  • A tiny, heavy BELL with TENSION fully up.
  • A FLAT RIDE with FLANGE at +25 degrees.

Notes and limits

  • The high-frequency wash above the coupled core is statistical, not mode by mode; the coupled core is exact for a shallow shell.
  • Decay times are calibrated to typical measured values per type (for example, a ride rings about 8 s at 1 kHz).
  • The stand swing is modelled as a slow rotation of the cymbal under the mic, not a full tilt.
  • The bow and brush are physical models of their own kind. They are quieter than stick hits, so the output is lifted by about 15 dB while PLAY is BOW.
  • Changing geometry costs a rebuild of about a second on a background thread; SIZE, HAMMER and everything in the sound and playing rows never do.
