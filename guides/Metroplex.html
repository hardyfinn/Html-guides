Metroplex

Overview

Each of the 16 stages has a pitch slider and a pulse count, and the stages share one sequence. Around that sit Mutate (a seeded, reversible way to vary the pattern), 8 snapshots, slides (including Acid), skips, scale quantising, a menu display, and four CTRL knobs that can be pointed at almost any setting.

The way it works follows the Intellijel Metropolix, but Metroplex is an independent module written from scratch. It is not made by or connected with Intellijel.

First patch

  1. Add Metroplex, a VCO, an ADSR envelope and a VCA.
  2. PITCH to the VCO's V/Oct. GATE to the ADSR's gate. VCO to the VCA input, ADSR to the VCA CV, VCA to your audio out.
  3. Leave CLOCK unpatched. Metroplex then runs on its own clock at 120 BPM, with 4 pulses per beat (each pulse is a 16th note).
  4. Move a few sliders. Set stage 4's PULSES to 3 and its GATE to multi (yellow) and it plays three 16ths in a row. Set stage 8 to hold (blue) with 4 pulses for a long note.

To sync with Live, patch a 16th-note clock into CLOCK and Live's transport reset into RESET.

Stage controls (one column per stage)

ControlWhat it does
PITCH sliderThe stage's note. The slider covers the Slider range (1 to 4 octaves) and always lands on a note in the chosen scale. Hover to see the note name.
PULSES (1 to 8)How many clock pulses the stage lasts.
GATE buttonClick to cycle the gate type: dark red = Rest (no gate), green = Single (one gate on the first pulse), yellow = Multi (a gate on every pulse), blue = Hold (one long gate across all the stage's pulses).
SLIDE buttonGlide into this stage's note. The Slide page sets how.
MUT buttonLit means Mutate may change this stage. Off means the stage is locked and always plays as set.
SKIP button (red)The stage is left out completely, as if it wasn't there. If every stage is skipped, the sequence keeps time in silence.

PULSES (all stages), the small knob at the top right, sets every stage's pulse count at once. It overwrites all 16 PULSES knobs, which you can then change one by one again.

The slider's light shows the playing stage. Stages outside the playing window (Start stage and Length) are dimmer.

The display and its menus

Six page buttons choose what the display shows: HOME, CLOCK, SEQ, SCALE, SLIDE and MUTATE. Each CTRL knob has its own menu button too.

  • Change the highlighted row: drag the DATA encoder up or down, or scroll over it. Hold Shift for steps of 10 (handy for tempo).
  • Next row: click the encoder or press the page button again. You can also click a row on the display, or drag on it.
  • On HOME, turning the encoder flips through the pages.
  • Right-click the display for a menu of everything on that page. The module's own right-click menu has every page.
  • A cyan C1 or C2 badge on a row means that CTRL knob is setting it right now. You can't change it from the menu until the knob is unassigned.

Mutate

Mutate varies the pattern without losing it. The MUTATE knob goes from 0 (exactly as programmed) to fully mutated. Each step up changes a few more stages: a note moves by some scale degrees, a pulse count or gate type changes, and (if allowed on the MUTATE page) slides and skips flip. Turn the knob back and the changes undo in reverse order, so 0 is always your original pattern.

The changes come from a seed. The same seed and knob position always give the same result. The yellow SEED button, or a trigger at the SEED jack, picks a new seed. Stages with MUT off are never changed. Put a CTRL knob on Mutate amount to sweep it with CV.

Snapshots

There are 8 snapshots. Each keeps the 16 stages (pitch, pulses, gate, slide, MUT, skip), Start stage, Length, Play order and the Mutate seed. Scale, tempo and the CTRL knobs stay the same across snapshots.

  • Save: press SAVE (red, it blinks), then a number button. Press SAVE again to cancel.
  • Recall: press a number button. With Snapshot switch on End of cycle (the default) it waits for the current pass to finish. Now switches at once.
  • SNAP jack: 0 to 7 V picks snapshot 1 to 8 (1 V each). A CTRL knob set to Snapshot does the same.
  • Right-click the module to clear all snapshots.

Slides

Turn on SLIDE on a stage to glide into its note from the one before.

  • Tempo: the glide takes the same time whatever the interval. Slide amount is that time as a share of one pulse (100% = a full pulse).
  • Analog: the glide moves at a fixed speed, so big jumps take longer, like a slew limiter. 100% = 2 seconds per octave, 25% = half a second per octave.
  • Acid: 303 style. A stage with SLIDE on glides out of itself into the next stage that plays, at the Analog speed. Its gate is held across all its pulses and tied into the next note with no gap, so an envelope doesn't retrigger, even if the stage's GATE is set to rest.

CTRL knobs

Each of the four CTRL knobs can be pointed at a setting with its menu button and the DATA encoder, or by right-clicking the knob. Each has a CV jack along the bottom and a CV amt attenuverter under the knob: fully right, +-5 V moves the knob across its whole range; noon means the CV does nothing; left inverts it. New modules start with CTRL 1 on Gate length, CTRL 2 on Slide amount, and CTRL 3 and 4 unassigned.

  • Direct: the knob is the setting: Tempo, Clock div, Gate length, Start stage, Length, Play order, Scale, Slider range, Slide amount, Mutate pitch and Mutate rhythm.
  • Offset: noon does nothing, left subtracts, right adds. Pitch pre (+-12 semitones, then back into the scale), Pitch post (+-12 semitones after the scale, a plain transpose), Octave (+-2), Pulse count (+-7 pulses on every stage).
  • Mutate amount: noon does nothing, left takes from the MUTATE knob, right adds to it.
  • Snapshot: the knob's travel is split into 8, picking snapshots 1 to 8.

If two knobs set the same direct setting, the higher-numbered CTRL wins. Two offsets on the same destination add together.

Patch ideas

  1. Evolving line. Program a riff, turn MUT off on the stages that hold it together (say 1, 5, 9, 13) and set CTRL 1 to Mutate amount. A slow LFO into CTRL 1's CV makes the line drift away and come back. Press SEED for fresh variations and SAVE one you like.
  2. Arrangement from knobs. CTRL 1 on Length, CTRL 2 on Pulse count, CTRL 3 on Start stage. Start at length 4 for an intro, open to 16 for the main part, then add pulses for a stretched breakdown. Record the moves as automation to replay the arrangement.
  3. Acid-style bass. Slide type Acid, amount 30%. Mostly Single gates, slides on two or three stages, and one or two SKIPs to shorten the bar. Pitch pre on CTRL 2 shifts the riff through the scale for the next section.
  4. Converging chords. Play order Converge on 8 stages, sent to a polyphonic or chord voice. The outside-in order gives a call-and-response shape from a plain riff.
