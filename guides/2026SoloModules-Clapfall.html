What it is

Clapfall makes percussion out of many tiny impacts. Every trigger releases a burst of short

"particles": a single hand clap, a finger snap, a stick tap, a coin, or the noise pulse inside a drum

machine's clap. How many particles arrive, how long the burst lasts and how tightly they line up

decide what you hear. A few particles in strict time make a classic 808 or 909 clap. A few hundred

particles a second with loose timing become a room of people clapping on 2 and 4. Leave it running

and it turns into applause.

None of it is sample playback. Each particle is modelled: a short burst of noise strikes a small set of

resonances that stand for the object. The hand claps were fitted to measurements of real claps (flat

palms ring near 1.9 kHz, an ordinary clap near 1.6 kHz, cupped hands near 700 Hz), and every

surface has 24 slightly different versions, so no two particles in a row are the same. The 808 and 909

surfaces copy the drum machines' trick of three fast noise pulses followed by a longer one.

Quick start

  1. Patch a clock or sequencer gate into TRIG and OUT L to your mixer (L alone gives a mono mix).
  2. Leave everything at default and press HIT: one person clapping a quick flam.
  3. Turn DENSITY up for more people, DECAY up for a longer burst, LOOSE up to make them less

together.

  1. Turn SURFACE to 808 and LOOSE fully left for a drum-machine clap that is identical every time.

Controls

Every knob has a CV input below it in the jack field. CV adds to the knob; 5 V turns a knob through its

whole range (so 1 V is a fifth of a turn). Hover over any control in Rack for a one-line reminder.

ControlWhat it does
SURFACEWhat each particle is. 22 built-in surfaces plus USER (your own samples). The name shows on the screen. CV of 5 V sweeps the whole list, so a sequencer can play a different surface on every step. A change applies to the next particle; ones already sounding finish.
DENSITYParticles per second, from 1.5 to 600. Think "how many people". Around 20 is one person, 100 to 200 is a handful, 400 and up is a crowd.
DECAYHow long a burst lasts after a trigger, from 12 ms to 6 s. In FREE mode it sets the cloud's level or chance directly (see FREE).
LOOSEFrom EXACT to HUMAN. Fully left, nothing is random: the particles come on a strict clock with fixed loudness, so every hit is identical, like a drum machine. Turned up, timing becomes as irregular as real people and loudness varies. Most of the change happens in the first quarter of the turn for loudness and across the whole turn for timing.
TONECentre of the band-pass filter, 150 Hz to 10 kHz. In the PITCH and PUNCH filter modes it also moves the pitch of the particles, two octaves either way (noon is natural pitch).
SPREADHow far particles scatter in pitch (up to 1.5 octaves either way) and across the stereo field. Low settings sound like one person; high settings like people all around you. Very high settings smear into noise.
REVERBSend into the built-in room / hall. The dry sound is never turned down. The reverb keeps ringing when the send closes, so an envelope into the REVERB CV jack that opens just after the hit gives a late, separate tail.
SIZESmall room (0.25 s) to big hall (3.2 s), with darker, later reflections as it grows.
ENVWhere the burst's envelope goes. AMP: particles keep coming at a steady rate and each one is
switchquieter, a smooth fade. BOTH: particles get quieter and rarer, a punchy burst with a gentle tail. CHANCE: particles keep their loudness but become rarer, the burst thins out.
FILTERBAND: band-pass only; TONE sets the colour. PITCH: band-pass, and TONE also moves the
switch HIT button Same as a trigger at TRIG.particles' pitch. PUNCH: the dry sound plus the band-pass on top, for a fuller, harder clap; TONE moves pitch. PUNCH is the default.
FREE buttonFree-running cloud (lights yellow), see below.
Particle lightRed light beside the screen blinks on every particle.

Jacks

JackWhat it does
TRIGStarts a burst. The first particle sounds on the trigger itself, so it is tight to the beat. Threshold 2 V.
ACCENTIf this is high (above 2 V) when a trigger arrives, that burst is 1.5 times louder and its envelope 1.6 times longer. Patch the sequencer's accent gate here.
ONEPlays a single particle at full level on each trigger, separate from any burst. Good for rimshots, ticks and ghost notes.
FREEWhile this gate is above 2 V the module runs free, as if the FREE button were on.
V/OCT1 V per octave pitch for the particles. When patched it replaces TONE's pitch role in every filter mode (TONE still moves the filter). 0 V is natural pitch.
SURF ... SIZECV for the eight knobs, 5 V = full turn.
OUT L /Stereo out, about 5 V peak for a single particle, soft-limited at 10 V. If only OUT L is patched it
OUT Rcarries a mono mix of both sides.

FREE mode: continuous clouds

With FREE on, there is no burst envelope. Particles run all the time at DENSITY, and DECAY takes the

envelope's place: with ENV on AMP it sets the level, on CHANCE it sets how likely each particle is, on

BOTH it does both. At DECAY fully left the cloud is silent, so DECAY becomes a "how much" control

you can ride by hand or CV. A trigger or HIT in FREE mode doesn't start a burst; it briefly pushes

DENSITY up (a surge inside the cloud). ONE still works.

Right-click menu

  • Same every hit: every burst replays the same random choices, even with LOOSE up. Useful

when you want a loose, human clap that is still repeatable. (With LOOSE fully left this happens

anyway.)

  • Load sample folder...: pick a folder of WAV files (up to 64, each trimmed to 2 s). They become

the USER surface, the last SURFACE position. The folder is remembered with the patch. Clear

sample folder removes it.

Patch recipes

Knob positions are given in percent of a full turn (0 % fully left, 50 % noon, 100 % fully right). Switch

positions are named. All of these assume TRIG from a sequencer and a stereo out.

808 clap

SURFACE 808, DENSITY 69 %, DECAY 32 %, LOOSE 0 %, TONE 47 %, SPREAD 0 %, ENV CHANCE,

FILTER PUNCH, REVERB 12 %, SIZE 20 %. Exactly three pulses and a tail, identical every time. Push

ACCENT on the last beat of the bar for the louder, longer version.

909 clap

SURFACE 909, DENSITY 67 %, DECAY 38 %, LOOSE 0 %, TONE 53 %, SPREAD 0 %, ENV CHANCE, FILTER PUNCH, REVERB 20 %, SIZE 30 %.

Late reverb clap (house)

The 909 recipe with REVERB at 0 % and SIZE 85 %. Patch an envelope (attack about 50 ms, hold 150

ms) triggered by the same gate into the REVERB CV jack. The dry clap stays dry and a big tail blooms

just after it.

One person

SURFACE CLAP, DENSITY 43 %, DECAY 0 %, LOOSE 50 %, TONE 56 %, SPREAD 5 %, ENV BOTH,

FILTER PUNCH, REVERB 25 %, SIZE 20 %. One clap, occasionally a natural flam.

Five people on 2 and 4 (soul, funk, hip-hop)

SURFACE GROUP, DENSITY 79 %, DECAY 26 %, LOOSE 100 %, TONE 55 %, SPREAD 22 %, ENV CHANCE, FILTER PUNCH, REVERB 30 %, SIZE 30 %.

Gospel room

SURFACE GROUP, DENSITY 94 %, DECAY 36 %, LOOSE 100 %, TONE 53 %, SPREAD 30 %, ENV CHANCE, FILTER PUNCH, REVERB 50 %, SIZE 55 %.

Applause

SURFACE GROUP, FREE on, DENSITY 35 % rising to 95 %, DECAY 5 % rising to 100 %, LOOSE 100 %,

TONE 58 %, SPREAD 40 %, ENV CHANCE, FILTER PUNCH, REVERB 35 %, SIZE 70 %. Ride DECAY

and DENSITY together (by hand, or one slow envelope into both CV jacks) to swell the crowd and let

it die away. Hits on TRIG make a surge of extra clapping.

Crowd into the drop (techno, house)

The Applause recipe with FREE driven by a gate that opens four bars before the drop and a slow ramp

into DECAY CV. Close the gate on the drop.

Snaps

SURFACE SNAP, DENSITY 62 %, DECAY 15 %, LOOSE 90 %, TONE 71 %, SPREAD 12 %, ENV BOTH, FILTER PUNCH, REVERB 30 %, SIZE 30 %.

Rimshot and clap from one module (reggae, hip-hop)

Clap settings of your choice on TRIG; SURFACE CV switching to RIM for the off-beats, or simply patch

a second gate into ONE with SURFACE on RIM and use another Clapfall for the clap. ONE ignores

DECAY, so the rim stays a single click.

Layered clap and snare

Mult the snare's trigger to TRIG. GROUP or PALMS, DENSITY around 60 %, DECAY 20 %, LOOSE 60

%, FILTER BAND, TONE around 60 %. Mix it under the snare; it adds the human edge without stealing

the body.

Percussion line from SURFACE

Sequence the SURF CV jack (steps of about 0.23 V move one surface) with LOOSE at 0 % and

DECAY near 0 %. Each step plays a different object.

Stick roll

SURFACE STICKS, DENSITY 49 %, DECAY 60 %, LOOSE 15 %, TONE 67 %, SPREAD 15 %, ENV AMP, FILTER PUNCH, REVERB 20 %, SIZE 30 %.

Glass melody

SURFACE GLASS, DENSITY 23 %, DECAY 63 %, LOOSE 20 %, SPREAD 2 %, ENV AMP, FILTER

PUNCH, REVERB 35 %, SIZE 60 %, and a sequencer's pitch into V/OCT.

Rain

SURFACE DROP, DENSITY 55 %, DECAY 89 % (or FREE with DECAY around 50 %), LOOSE 100 %, TONE 50 %, SPREAD 60 %, ENV CHANCE, FILTER PITCH, REVERB 40 %, SIZE 60 %.

Vinyl crackle bed

SURFACE CRACKLE, FREE on, DENSITY 47 %, DECAY 50 %, LOOSE 100 %, TONE 75 %, SPREAD 20

%, ENV CHANCE, FILTER BAND, REVERB 0 %. Turn it well down in the mix under drums.

Dropped coins, ping-pong

COINS: DENSITY 64 %, DECAY 60 %, LOOSE 100 %, SPREAD 35 %, ENV CHANCE. PING: DENSITY

30 %, DECAY 86 %, LOOSE 30 %, ENV AMP: a ball bouncing slower and slower is the next thing to

try, with DENSITY falling by CV during the burst.

Your own samples

Right-click, Load sample folder..., and choose a folder of short WAVs: claps you recorded, hits, foley.

Up to 64 files are read (in name order), mixed to mono, cut to 2 seconds and normalised. Turn

SURFACE fully right to USER and they play exactly like the built-in surfaces: never the same one twice

running, scattered by SPREAD, pitched by TONE or V/OCT. The screen shows how many files were

loaded. Short, dry, single hits work best.

Notes and limits

  • The hands and the 808 were tuned against measurements of real recordings. The other

surfaces (PING, SWITCH, COINS and so on) are modelled from general acoustics and are

plausible rather than exact copies of a particular object.

  • The module has no audio input, gives stereo out, is monophonic (one burst engine), and uses

about 1 to 2 % of one CPU core even in dense applause.

  • Clapfall is inspired by the idea of multi-particle clap synthesis, best known from WMD's

Fracture Eurorack module. It has its own name, panel, particle models and code, and shares no

sounds or artwork with that module.
