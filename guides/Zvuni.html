Zvuni

Overview

  • Zvuni (“it rings”) is a three-voice singer: a drone, a melody, and a partner that sits a microtonal second away from the melody. The partner is tuned to a target roughness (how fast the two voices beat) instead of a fixed interval, the way village singers tune by ear. One knob, TENSION, goes from unison through the ringing second to an open fifth.
  • Aksak is a clock divider for Bulgarian “limping” meters: 2+2+3, 2+2+2+3, 2+2+3+2+2 and your own.

Everything is synthesised, so there is nothing to clear if you use it on a record.

Quick start

  1. Patch a pitch (V/oct) and gate into MELODY and GATE at the bottom left. Leave DRONE empty for now: the drone takes the first note of each phrase.
  2. Listen to MIX L / R.
  3. Turn TENSION slowly from fully left. At about 60% the partner reaches the “peak ring”. Past that it opens out, and fully right it is a fifth.
  4. Clock Aksak with 16ths, patch its ACCENT into Zvuni's ACCENT and its END into RESOLVE. Turn up CLING: the melody leans onto ringing notes on the strong beats, and every cycle ends on a cadence.

The idea

Two tones beat when they are close in pitch. The ear finds that beating roughest at about a quarter of a “critical band” apart, roughly 25–35 beats a second in the singing range. So the interval singers land on depends on pitch:

Melody noteGap for peak ringAs an interval
A3 (220 Hz)~26 Hz~193 cents
A4 (440 Hz)~28 Hz~108 cents
E5 (660 Hz)~32 Hz~83 cents

In Hz mode Zvuni does the same: as the melody climbs, the partner moves closer in cents so the rub stays equally strong. Cents mode uses a fixed interval so you can hear the difference.

The display

  • Left: roughness curve for the note the melody is singing. Left edge = unison, then semitone, tone, 4th, 5th (small intervals get more room). The gold dot is the partner; the small red mark is where RING puts the peak.
  • Right: melody and drone notes, the partner's offset in cents, beat speed, the mode, and RESOLVING or YELP! when they happen. With several channels it shows channel 1.
  • RING bar: how hard melody and partner ring now (same as the ROUGH output).

Aksak

ControlWhat it does
METER5 (2+3), 7 (2+2+3) Rachenitsa, 7 (3+2+2), 9 (2+2+2+3) Daychovo, 11 (2+2+3+2+2) Kopanitsa, 13, 15, or Custom. A change waits for the end of the current cycle. CV: each 1.25 V moves one meter on.
MUTATEChance each cycle that one group flips between 2 and 3 (smaller chance for a second). The base meter returns every time. CV 0–10 V.
CUSTOM GROUPSEight buttons used when METER is Custom. Tap to cycle off > 2 (green) > 3 (red). Empty groups are skipped.
CLOCK inOne pulse per step (16ths work well).
RESET inThe next clock starts the cycle again.
STEP outA trigger on every clock.
ACCENT outA trigger at the start of each group.
LONG outA trigger at the start of each group of 3.
END outA gate high during the last group of the cycle.

The display shows the cycle as boxes, with a gap between groups and a red mark on groups of 3.

Patch ideas

  • Tension as arrangement: automate TENSION: verses near unison, the build climbing to the peak, the drop snapping out to a fifth with RESOLVE.
  • Roughness drives the track: ROUGH into a filter cutoff or a kick sidechain amount, so the rest reacts as the voices rub.
  • Aksak hats over a four-on-the-floor kick: Aksak on 7 drives hats from STEP and an open hat from LONG. Bring MUTATE up slowly.
  • Tuning brain: PTR V/O and MEL V/O to other oscillators; turn Zvuni's own levels down.
  • Hear why singers do it: play a melody that climbs an octave and flip MODE between Hz and Cents.

Notes

  • The roughness model is the standard critical-band approximation, not a measurement of any particular singer. Interval figures are ballpark values from the ethnomusicology literature.
