What it is

Cymbolism Lite is Cymbolism with the four most expensive ranges narrowed and a lighter engine. The physics, the panel, the jacks and the sound inside the shared range are the same. It exists so that you can press Randomize, or throw random CV at every jack, and never land on a cymbal that swamps the CPU.

What Lite leaves out:

  • Very big cymbals. SIZE tops out at 1.2x the type's size instead of 1.5x. A bigger cymbal has more modes under 20 kHz, and every mode costs.
  • Paper-thin cymbals. WEIGHT starts at 0.8x instead of 0.5x. Thin metal also packs more modes into the audible band, and the thinnest settings took several seconds to build.
  • Half the rivets. SIZZLE goes to 6 instead of 12.
  • The bow. Bowing keeps a beater in continuous contact with the shell, the most expensive way to play it. The struck beaters and the brush remain.
  • HIGH and ULTRA. Lite runs MID (the default) or ECO.

Everything else is unchanged and keeps its full range: TYPE, TAPER, PROFILE, BELL, FLANGE, ALLOY, HAMMER, TENSION, DECAY, BLOOM, DAMP, MOUNT, MIC, POSITION, FORCE, HARDNESS, RATE and EXC. Those were measured and either cost little or their limits saved almost nothing. For the full description of how the cymbal is modelled, see the Cymbolism manual.

How much lighter

120 random settings, made the way Rack's Randomize makes them, each struck every half second. The figures are percent of one core of a cloud machine; an Apple silicon Mac will read lower, but the proportions hold.

ModuleAverage1 in 10 exceedsWorst
Cymbolism, HIGH44%64%83%
Cymbolism Lite, MID31%46%52%
Cymbolism Lite, ECO23%34%40%

So Lite at MID costs about 30% less on average and nearly 40% less in the worst case; ECO about half. The absolute worst corner, a thin crash or china at the biggest, thinnest, flattest setting with full flange, falls from 111% (Cymbolism, HIGH, at its limits) to 64% (Lite, MID) and 52% (Lite, ECO). A silent cymbal still costs nothing, and the cost falls under 10% once a ring has faded.

Randomize also turns TYPE, and turning TYPE loads that type's shape into the geometry knobs unless "Keep geometry when TYPE changes" is ticked. The table is for the default. With it ticked, the geometry knobs are randomized too, which gives wilder cymbals: the average falls a little (23% at MID) but the worst case rises to about 57% at MID, against 85% for Cymbolism at HIGH.

Controls

Ranges that differ from Cymbolism are in bold.

Cymbal (changing these rebuilds the modes, about a second)

SIZELite: 0.6x to 1.2x the type's size (Cymbolism goes to 1.5x). Diameter, shown in inches. Size is an exact scale of the whole cymbal, so it needs no rebuild and can be modulated freely. Its CV is 1 V/oct: +1 V halves the size and raises every pitch an octave. CV can shrink the cymbal further but never makes it bigger than 1.2x.

WEIGHT Lite: 0.8x to 2.5x the type's thickness (Cymbolism goes down to 0.5x). Thickness. Thin is low, washy, quick to bloom and very nonlinear; heavy is higher, pingy and glassy.

TAPERCentre thickness over edge thickness. More taper gives a crashy edge around a solid, rideable centre.
PROFILEHow high the bow rises. Flat is dry and controlled; high is brighter, with more pitch glide and a stiffer low end.
BELLBell size and height: from no bell to a big, loud, pitched bell.
FLANGEEdge turned down (negative) or up (positive, china). Large effect on trashiness.
ALLOYB20 is dark and long; B12 and B8 are brighter and shorter; brass and nickel silver sound cheaper; steel is harsh and long.
HAMMERFrom a perfect machine-spun disc to a hand-hammered one: splits mode pairs (slow beating), detunes modes and twists their orientation. No rebuild. NEW CYMBAL re-rolls the hammering pattern: a different cymbal of the same model. It is saved with the patch.
TENSIONResidual stress from hammering and lathing. Positive stiffens and raises the low modes; negative loosens them. Too much negative would buckle the cymbal, so it is limited automatically.

Sound (no rebuild)

DECAY0.25x to 4x the natural ring time.
BLOOMThe nonlinearity: 0 is a linear, glassy, bell-like cymbal; 1 is physical; 2 is an exaggerated, roaring wash.
DAMPTape or a gel pad on the bow.
MOUNTTight felt (shorter, drier, damped lows) to loose (longer low ring, and the cymbal swings on its stand after a hit, which slowly shifts the tone at the mic).

SIZZLE Lite: 0 to 6 rivets (Cymbolism has 12). The first rivet costs the most CPU; the rest add little.

MICA continuous morph: BELL (close over the bell), BOW (close over mid-bow, default), EDGE (bright and trashy), OVERHEAD (about 1 m up), ROOM (3 m away, with air absorption). Each position is matched in loudness.

Playing

PLAYLite: TIP, SHOULDER, MALLET, RODS, BRUSH (no bow). The PLAY CV (0 to 10 V across the five) is added and read at each strike.
POSITIONWhere the beater lands: bell (0) to edge (1). The bell gives a clear bell tone; the edge drives the full crash.
FORCEStrike speed, 0.2 to 12 m/s, when VEL is unpatched; with VEL patched, the speed at 10 V. It is also the EXCITE gain.
HARDNESSBeater stiffness within its type: soft wood to nylon, soft yarn to hard felt, soft to stiff brush wires.
RATE2 to 30 Hz: roll speed for the struck beaters, sweep speed for BRUSH.
EXCHow much of the EXCITE input is pushed into the cymbal at the strike point.
STRIKE / CHOKEHit now; grab the cymbal while held.

Jacks

STRIKE (trigger), VEL (0 to 10 V, read at the trigger), CHOKE (gate), ROLL (gate: rolls with the struck beaters, a sweep with BRUSH), EXCITE (audio), PLAY, and CV for every knob that has a jack (CV adds to the knob; 10 V covers the knob's range, and the result is held inside the Lite limits). OUT is mono, about 2 to 3 V peak for a medium crash hit; very hard hits are softly limited above 8 V.

Context menu

Quality:

  • MID (the default): 56 nonlinear core modes with coupled upper pairs and the full band to 20 kHz.
  • ECO: 48 core modes and a 16 kHz band. The lightest; the top octave is a little duller.

The other menu items are as on Cymbolism: Run on its own thread, Keep geometry when TYPE changes, Rebuild on geometry CV changes, Load the type's playing defaults, OUT trim.

Patch recipes

1. Randomize jam

  • Setup: a clock into STRIKE, and a second, slower clock (every 4 or 8 bars) into a module that can randomize another module, or press Randomize by hand on the bar line.
  • Why Lite: every result stays light enough to leave several running at once. Tick "Keep geometry when TYPE changes" for stranger cymbals.

2. Random CV kit

  • Setup: a trigger sequencer into STRIKE and VEL, and sample-and-hold of noise (clocked by the same triggers) into SIZE, BLOOM, POSITION and MIC.
  • Result: each hit is a slightly different cymbal. SIZE needs no rebuild, so this is instant and free. CV cannot push SIZE above 1.2x, so it cannot surprise the CPU either.

3. Rod roll swell

  • Setup: preset "Rod Roll", or CRASH RIDE, RODS, RATE about 14 Hz, MIC OVERHEAD.
  • Playing: hold ROLL and ramp VEL from 0 to 10 V over a bar or two; release ROLL and let the wash ring.

4. Two rides on one core

  • Setup: two Lite modules, one RIDE with TIP at POSITION 55% and one HEAVY RIDE on the bell (POSITION 8%, MIC BELL), both at ECO.
  • Playing: a swing pattern into the first, accents into the second. Together they cost about what one Cymbolism at HIGH did.

5. Brush and choke

  • Setup: RIDE, BRUSH, RATE 5 to 8 Hz, POSITION about 60%, DAMP a little.
  • Playing: gate ROLL for sweeps, triggers to STRIKE for taps, and a short gate into CHOKE at the end of each phrase.

Notes

  • Lite and Cymbolism share their engine, so a Lite setting sounds exactly as the same setting does on Cymbolism at the same quality.
  • Patches made with Cymbolism do not load into Lite; they are separate modules. Copy the knob values by hand, or use a Lite preset as the start.
  • Changing geometry costs a rebuild of about a second on a background thread; SIZE, HAMMER and everything in the sound and playing rows never do.
