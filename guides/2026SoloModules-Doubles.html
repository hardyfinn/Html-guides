What it is

Beat juggling is the hip-hop DJ craft of playing two copies of the same record on two decks and using the crossfader and both hands to loop, double, chase and rebuild a break. Doubles does this to a short piece you record into it. Both decks are modelled turntables. A record takes time to start, pulling it back takes time, and the hands that cue it are not perfect. The HANDS section sets how perfect they are.

Signal flow

Input → RECORD (1 to 16 bars, starting on a bar line) → the record → deck A and deck B (two copies; B is a little worn, set by COPY) → crossfader → soft limiter → LEVEL → output. With no record, or while recording, the input passes straight through. Once a record exists, the device is the sound source and the input is ignored.

Fast passages get looser. Heavy hands: slower starts, a small push or drag on each release, slower pull-backs.
SLOP RISK COPYMissed cue points (worse the further the pull), a sliver of pull-back through the fader, uneven levels, a trembling held record. How often a hit is fluffed outright. How worn the second copy is: a touch slower, duller and quieter. The decks drift apart and get nudged back.
PLAYMODE DENSITY, FUNK, ARC ROUTINE...HANDS-ON: you play the buttons. ROUTINE: the 16 slots play in turn. INSTINCT: the device chooses. Instinct: how busy, how syncopated, and over how many bars it builds up. Opens the 16 slots, SLOT LENGTH (bar or half bar) and RESET.
OUTSTRAIGHT LEVEL, BRAKELevel of the plain record between moves. At 0 you hear only the moves. Output level. BRAKE: on stop, the record is heard slowing down.

Recipes

  1. Extend a break. Record 2 bars of the break. Leave MODE on HANDS-ON. Turn LOOP on at 1/2 for the last bar of each 4, then DOUBLE at 1/8 for a bar. Turn it off before the drop.
  2. Half-time breakdown. CHASE at 1/4 for 4 bars, then SHUFFLE at 50 % for 2. With HANDS at 70 it loosens up like a real chase.
  3. Fill generator over the original. Keep the original clip playing on another track. Record it into Doubles, set STRAIGHT to 0, MODE to INSTINCT, DENSITY 40. Doubles only speaks during its moves.
  4. New beat from two hits. Map pads 1 and 2 to two keys or pads. Turn STRAIGHT down and play your own pattern from the kick and snare. FILL plays the written patterns the same way.
  5. Flanged bar. PHASE on for one bar before a drop. Raise COPY for a wider sweep.
  6. A written routine. Open ROUTINE... and set slots 1 to 16, for example STRAIGHT, LOOP, DOUBLE, CHASE, STRAIGHT, TRIPLE, FILL 2, SPIN. Set MODE to ROUTINE and press RESET a bar before you want it to start.
  7. Juggle something else. Record a bass line, a vocal phrase or a horn stab instead of drums. Doubles and chases work on anything rhythmic. Turn FILL KICK and SNARE to fixed stickers.
  8. Clean slicer. HANDS to 0 and COPY to 0: every cut is sample-exact and nothing drifts. Good for edits you then resample.
  9. Practice-room sound. HANDS to 100 with RISK at 30: late releases, slips and the odd fluffed hit.

Playing it from a controller

An audio effect does not receive MIDI notes, so use Live's MIDI Map mode. Press Cmd-M, click a button or pad on Doubles, press a key or pad on your controller, then press Cmd-M again. The move buttons and pads can also be automated in an arrangement, which is how a routine can be drawn in.

Good to know

Doubles assumes 4/4. Changing the tempo after recording repitches the record, like a turntable. The record is saved as a WAV next to the device, and the Live set remembers which one. If that file is moved, the strip shows RECORD MISSING. Several Doubles in one set each keep their own record.
