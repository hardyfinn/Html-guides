The voice box (syrinx). A bird sings with a pair of small membranes, one on each side of its airway.

Air pressure from the lungs sets them flapping, and muscles set their tension. Birdie runs the same

physics, a small set of equations from the birdsong research literature, sixteen times per sample. Two

controls matter most:

  • PRESSURE starts the sound and makes it louder and brighter.
  • TENSION sets the pitch, and also the tone. High tension is a pure whistle. Turn it down and the

pitch falls while the tone turns spiky and buzzy. Right at the bottom the voice breaks into rough

pulses, which is where crows, ducks and the bass sounds live.

The body. After the voice box, the sound goes through the windpipe (TRACHEA and TUBE), the throat

(THROAT and TUNE) and the beak (BEAK). Real songbirds move their throat to follow their pitch, and

that is what makes a whistle clean, so FOLLOW is on by default.

A note is a gesture: a swell of pressure plus a slide in tension, shaped by SHAPE, SLIDE, ATTACK and

RELEASE. BUZZ wobbles it fast.

A phrase is a family of notes. The note you set on the panel is always note type A. Birdie invents three

relatives, B, C and D, from the seed. VARY decides how different they are. PATTERN decides the

order. RATE, DRIFT, ACCEL and SWELL shape the whole phrase. The same seed always gives the

same phrase, so a good one can be kept with the patch.

SIZE scales the whole bird. Pitch follows it exactly: twice the size is one octave down. The SIZE input

is 1 V per octave, so a sequencer can play the bird in tune.

The panel

The display shows the pattern and, while a phrase plays, which note it is on (for example MOTIF

3/6 ). On the right it shows FREE or LOCK and the last four digits of the seed. LOCK? means CLOCK

is at LOCK but no clock has arrived yet, so phrases run free. The light next to the display follows the

pressure.

Note

ControlWhat it does
LENGTHOne note's length, 10 ms to 4 s. In a phrase a note never runs past 92% of its slot.
SLIDEHow far the pitch moves during a note, -24 to +24 semitones. Negative falls, positive rises.
SHAPEThe curve of the slide: LINE, FALL (fast, then settling), SWOOP (slow, then fast), ARCH (out and back), DOUBLE (two arches).
ATTACK, RELEASEFade in and fade out, as a share of the note.
BUZZ RATESpeed of the buzz, 5 to 400 Hz.
BUZZ DEPTHHow far the buzz wobbles the tension.
CHOPHow hard the buzz chops the air, from tremolo to separate clicks.

Body and stretch

ControlWhat it does

TRACHEA Windpipe length, 0.5 to 10 cm at size 1.

TUBEHow strongly the windpipe resonates: hollow and nasal when high.
THROATHow much the throat filters the sound.
TUNEWhere the throat sits: a multiple of the pitch with FOLLOW on (0.5 to 4 times), or a fixed formant with it off.
FOLLOWON: the throat tracks the pitch (clean whistles). OFF: a fixed formant (harsh calls, vowel sounds).
BEAKBeak opening: low is muffled like a dove, high is bright.
TIMESlows every gesture, buzz and phrase up to 16 times, without changing pitch.
HOLDThe voice sounds all the time, like an oscillator. Notes and phrases still play their slides on top. The HOLD jack does the same while its gate is high.

Phrase and play

ControlWhat it does
NOTESNotes in a phrase, 1 to 32.
RATEFREE: notes per second, 0.25 to 40. LOCK: notes per clock pulse, in nine steps: 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8.

PATTERN The order of note types A, B, C and D (see below).

VARYHow different B, C and D are from A. At 0 every note is identical.
DRIFTPitch movement across the phrase, up to an octave either way.
ACCELThe phrase speeds up (positive) or slows down (negative) by its end.
SWELLThe phrase grows louder (positive) or fades away (negative).
GAPWith AUTO on, the rest between phrases, 0.1 to 8 s (stretched by TIME).
NOTEButton: sing one note.
PHRASEButton: sing one phrase.
NEWButton: a new seed, used from the next phrase.
AUTOON: the bird sings by itself, phrase after phrase, with GAP between. Triggers still work and restart the wait.
CLOCKFREE: phrases run at RATE. LOCK: Birdie measures the CLOCK input and fits RATE's number of notes into each clock.

Right-click menu. New seed every phrase gives a fresh phrase every time. Seed shows the seed and

lets you type one.

Patch recipes

A garden bird. Songster, AUTO on, and New seed every phrase in the menu. A slow random CV into

TENSION makes it wander.

Call and answer. Two Birdies. The first one's END into the second one's PHRASE, and the second

one's END back into the first one's PHRASE. Give them different SIZE and seeds.

Trill fills. Triller, with PHRASE from a sequencer's last step. Use ACCEL and DRIFT for rising or falling

fills.

Bass Bird. Load the preset. Send a quarter-note clock to CLOCK and a bar clock to PHRASE. Patch a

sequencer's pitch into SIZE (1 V/oct) for the notes, and an envelope into TENSION as the "filter": up

for clean, down for growl.

Acid bird. HOLD on, SIZE between 8 and 32, sequencer pitch through a slew into SIZE. An envelope

into TENSION and CHOP up a little. Use PRESS to duck a pad.

Dub siren. Siren, or Whistler with LENGTH long, an LFO into TENSION, and a delay after it.

Scratch birds. Chirper, NOTE from a drum pattern, a different SLIDE voltage on each step.

The bird as a modulator. PRESS and PITCH from Birdie into a filter cutoff or another oscillator's pitch.

GATE and END make rhythms.

Monsters. Any preset with SIZE and TIME turned up together.

Machines. Robot, ROUGH and CHOP by CV, FOLLOW off, and an LFO on TUNE for vowel sounds.

What to know

  • The voice model is the one used in birdsong research (Mindlin and colleagues). It was written

from memory and checked by running it: it starts at a pressure threshold, tension sets the pitch,

and the tone gets rich at low tension, as the papers describe. The windpipe, throat and beak

are simplified filters.

  • No preset is fitted to a recording of a real bird. They were judged by measurement and

spectrogram, not by ear.

  • CPU: about 4% of one core at 48 kHz on a cloud test machine. The voice box is solved 16 times

per sample at 44.1 and 48 kHz, 8 times at 88.2 and 96 kHz, and 4 times above, so the cost

stays roughly the same at any sample rate.

  • Changes from the design: RATE goes down to 0.25 notes per second and LENGTH up to 4 s, so

the Churr, Siren and Drone presets fit on the knobs. The 2ND VOICE INTERVAL has no CV jack

(there was no room in the row of twelve).
